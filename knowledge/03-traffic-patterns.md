# Traffic Patterns

A standard airport traffic pattern provides a predictable sequence of flight legs around the runway.

The standard legs are:

1. Upwind
2. Crosswind
3. Downwind
4. Base
5. Final

## Upwind

Upwind is the initial departure leg after takeoff.

The aircraft generally travels in the same direction as the runway landing direction.

## Crosswind

Crosswind is the leg perpendicular to the runway after the aircraft has departed the upwind leg.

## Downwind

Downwind is flown approximately parallel to the runway in the opposite direction of the landing direction.

The aircraft is moving away from the approach end of the runway while remaining offset from the runway.

## Base

Base is the leg between downwind and final.

It is approximately perpendicular to the runway.

## Final

Final is the last leg before landing.

The aircraft is aligned with the runway centerline and travelling toward the runway.

## Left and right traffic

A left traffic pattern uses left turns between the pattern legs.

A right traffic pattern uses right turns between the pattern legs.

If ATC specifies a left traffic pattern, turns should be to the left.

If ATC specifies a right traffic pattern, turns should be to the right.

## Position reports

A pilot may report:

- "Upwind"
- "Crosswind"
- "Downwind"
- "Base"
- "Final"

These are position reports.

A position report does not automatically constitute a request for a new instruction.

If the pilot was previously instructed to report a particular leg, reporting that leg satisfies the instruction.

For example:

ATC:
"Report downwind."

Pilot:
"LV-INQ, downwind."

The pilot has complied with the reporting instruction. ATC should not repeat "report downwind".

## Pattern altitude

Traffic-pattern altitude is determined by the airport, aircraft, and established simulation situation.

Do not invent a pattern altitude if it has not been provided by the simulation or established situation.

# Approach and Landing

Approach and landing operations follow the established traffic pattern and current airport situation.

## Approach sequence

For a standard traffic pattern, the aircraft normally progresses through:

- Downwind
- Base
- Final
- Landing

The aircraft's current telemetry and pilot position reports determine which part of the approach the aircraft is currently in.

## Downwind

Downwind is flown approximately parallel to the runway and opposite the landing direction.

A pilot reporting "downwind" is reporting their current position.

If ATC has instructed the pilot to report downwind and the pilot reports downwind, the reporting instruction has been completed.

Do not repeatedly instruct the pilot to report downwind.

## Base

Base is the leg between downwind and final.

It is approximately perpendicular to the runway.

A pilot reporting "base" is reporting their current position in the traffic pattern.

If ATC has instructed the pilot to report base and the pilot reports base, the reporting instruction has been completed.

## Final

Final is the final leg of the traffic pattern before landing.

The aircraft should be aligned with the runway centerline and travelling toward the runway.

A pilot reporting "final" is reporting their current position.

If ATC has instructed the pilot to report final and the pilot reports final, the reporting instruction has been completed.

## Landing clearance

"Cleared to land runway 23" authorizes the aircraft to land on the specified runway.

A landing clearance is different from an instruction to report final.

For example:

ATC:
"Report final runway 23."

Pilot:
"Final runway 23."

The pilot has reported their position. This does not by itself constitute a landing clearance.

ATC must separately issue a landing clearance when appropriate.

## Readback of landing clearance

A pilot may acknowledge a landing clearance by repeating it.

Example:

ATC:
"Cleared to land runway 23."

Pilot:
"Cleared to land runway 23, LV-INQ."

This is a readback, not a new request.

If the readback is correct and no further operational action is required, output:

[NO RESPONSE]

## Go-around

A go-around occurs when an aircraft discontinues an approach
and does not land.

A go-around may be initiated by the pilot or instructed by ATC.

After a go-around, the aircraft normally continues according
to the applicable missed-approach or traffic-pattern procedure.

## Position reports are not requests

A pilot reporting:

- "Downwind"
- "Base"
- "Final"
- "On final runway 23"

is normally providing position information.

Do not automatically issue a new clearance or instruction merely because the pilot reported a position.

Determine whether an operational response is actually required from the established situation and current aircraft state.

## Do not invent landing information

Do not invent:

- runway conditions
- wind
- altimeter setting
- approach frequencies
- traffic
- runway occupancy
- landing clearances
- approach restrictions
- altitudes

unless the information is provided by the simulation or established situation.
